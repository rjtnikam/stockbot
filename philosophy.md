# Rajat's Investing Philosophy (v1 — edit freely; every bot reads this before judging a stock)

## Who I am as an investor
- Self-directed investor in Indian small and mid caps. Roots: ValuePickr, and the frameworks of
  Donald Francis, Ayush Mittal and Hitesh Pabari.
- Mechanical engineer: I have an edge judging precision engineering and capital goods businesses.
  Use it — ask about capacity, process, tolerances, customer qualification cycles.

## What makes a good candidate
1. **A story that is changing.** Every stock has a story; I want the moment it turns
   (new capacity, new customers, margin structure change, balance sheet repair), ideally before
   the market believes it.
2. **Numbers that confirm the story.** A story without numbers is a promotion.
   Look for earnings inflection in the quarterlies, not in management's adjectives.
3. **Growth that is economic, not just big:** g = Reinvestment Rate × ROIIC (Mauboussin).
   Growth with low ROIIC destroys value.
4. **Quality over cheapness.** "Smart at one price, stupid at another." Price matters, but a great
   business at a fair price beats a fair business at a great price.
5. **Expectations, not forecasts.** Use reverse DCF: what growth does today's price imply? The
   opportunity is when delivered/likely growth is clearly above implied growth. Respect base rates:
   very few companies sustain 20%+ growth for 10 years.

## How I hunt (what the automated hunter looks for)
- **Numbers that turn:** revenue jump (YoY and QoQ), EPS acceleration, margin expansion (QoQ and YoY),
  profit inflection, turnaround. Operating leverage (margins rising faster than revenue) is a tell.
- **Capex ahead of demand:** gross block + CWIP expanding while revenue hasn't caught up yet. This is
  management betting before the market sees it. The trigger to watch is **commissioning /
  commercial production**: that is when capacity starts converting into revenue (Hidden → Inflection).
  Ask: who funded it (operating cash = strong; heavy debt = fragile; dilution = check price)?
- **Expensive signals** (costly-signalling: only a confident, honest management can afford them):
  promoters buying warrants/preferential shares at or above market; promoter open-market buying;
  capex funded before demand is visible; specific numeric guidance (costly if missed);
  voluntary disclosures beyond the rules; turning down business to protect margins.
- **Management behaviour:** guidance policy (specific / directional / refuses) matters less than the
  **say-do record**. A management that refuses guidance but keeps delivering is an under-promiser;
  one that guides big and misses quietly is a promoter. Candor = volunteering bad news first.
- **First-ever concall / first investor meet:** a small company starting to court institutions;
  combined with improving numbers it often marks the move from Hidden to Recognition.
- **Order wins only count relative to size:** order value / annual revenue. A stream of tiny order
  announcements is a promotion pattern, not a story.
- **Warrants cut both ways:** promoters paying at a premium = skin in the game; a long list of
  unrelated non-promoter allottees at a discount = possible operator play.
- **Change in control / new professional leadership** can restart a story; verify the acquirer.

## Hard quantitative rules (Buffett codex)
- **EBITDA is banned** as a proxy for earnings. Depreciation is a real cost.
- **Interest coverage = pre-interest pretax earnings / interest**, never EBITDA-based.
- **ROTNA:** 25%+ terrific; 12–20% good; below that needs a special reason.
- **Stock-based compensation is a real expense.** ~80% of acquisition amortisation is non-real.
- **Cash conversion:** CFO/PAT persistently well below ~0.8 is a question that must be answered
  (receivables build-up, reclassifications, capitalised costs).
- **Risk = probability of permanent loss of purchasing power**, not beta or volatility.
- **Capital allocation hierarchy:** reinvest at high returns > bolt-ons > acquisitions > buying other
  stocks > buybacks (only below intrinsic value) > dividends.

## Forensic red flags (cockroach theory — problems cluster)
- Receivables or inventory days rising faster than sales; receivables reclassified.
- Lease mechanics (Ind AS 116) flattering EBITDA/CFO; cash-flow restatements.
- Auditor changes or resignations, qualified opinions, related-party transactions, promoter pledges,
  falling promoter holding without explanation, frequent equity dilution.
- Profit growth with no matching cash flow. One cockroach means look for more.
- Statutory auditor resignation, defaults, forensic audits: the hunter removes the company outright.
  CFO / independent-director exits, pledges, downgrades, regulator action: heavy penalties.

## The timeline lens (where technicals help)
- Price and story move in stages: Hidden → Inflection → Recognition → Consensus → Decay.
- Best zone: story at Inflection/early Recognition, price still basing or early stage 2.
- Use technicals (Weinstein stage, RS, bases, volume dry-up) for **timing and confirmation**,
  never as the reason to own a business.
- Price ahead of story (Consensus with rich implied growth) = do not chase.

## Sell rules
- Don't thumb-suck (Tesco lesson): when the thesis breaks, act; don't wait for proof of disaster.
- Hold quality compounders unless implied expectations clearly outrun mine.
- Errors of omission cost more than errors of commission.

## My known failure modes (flag these whenever relevant)
1. **I exit quality compounders too early on price anchors.** Canonical lesson: APARINDS, sold at
   ₹1,237, later ~₹11,266. If a compounder is working and expectations aren't extreme, say "HOLD — anchor risk".
2. **I overstay cyclicals.** If the business is cyclical and the cycle is late, say so loudly.
